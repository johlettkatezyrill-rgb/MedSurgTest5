Test 5 Study Guide

Include Causes, Common Condition, Symptoms, Nursing Interventions for Respiratory Acidosis, Respiratory Alkalosis, Metabolic Acidosis and Metabolic Alkalosis.

  • Know the normal ranges for pH, CO2, and HCO3.
  • Be able to interpret ABG's.

Respiratory Acidosis

Causes
hypoventilation | CO2 retention
  • COPD, pneumonia, asthma attack, atelectasis
  • resp. depression (opioids, sedatives, anesthesia)
  • neuromuscular disease, head injury, obesity
  • airway obstruction, chest wall injury
Common Conditions
COPD, pneumonia, overdose with CNS depressants, Guillain-Barré
Symptoms
headache, hypoxia, drowsiness, confusion, lethargy, restlessness, tachycardia, hyperkalemia, dysrhythmias, coma.
Nursing Interventions
maintain airway, position in semi- to high Fowler's, encourage coughing & deep breathing (incentive spirometry). Give O2 cautiously and bronchodilators as prescribed. Reverse causes (naloxone for opioid overdose) avoid oversedation. Monitor: ABGs, SpO2, RR, LOC, cardiac rhythm
ABG:
pH7.35
CO₂above 45
HCO₃22-26 mEq/L

HCO₃: normal, high if compensating

Respiratory Alkalosis

Causes
hyperventilation | excess CO2 blown off
  • anxiety, panic attack, pain, fever
  • hypoxia, PE, pneumonia
  • overventilation
  • early salicylate (aspirin) OD, sepsis, high altitude
Common Conditions
anxiety/panic attacks, pulmonary embolism, pain, mechanical ventilation, aspirin toxicity.
Symptoms
rapid deep breathing, lightheadedness, dizziness, numbness and tingling (fingers, around mouth), muscle spasms/tetany, hyperreflexia, palpitations, anxiety, hypokalemia, hypocalcemia signs, seizures in severe cases
Nursing Interventions
stay with & calm the patient, coach slow breathing. Treat the cause (pain, anxiety, fever, hypoxia). Have the patient breathe slowly (paper bag). Provide O2 if hypoxic. Adjust ventilator setting as prescribed. Monitor ABGs, electrolytes (K, Ca), and cardiac rhythm; seizure precautions
ABG:
pH↑ 7.45
CO₂↓ 35
HCO₃22-26 mEq/L

HCO₃: normal, low if compensating

Metabolic Acidosis

Causes
Acid gain or bicarbonate loss, DKA, starvation, severe diarrhea, renal failure, lactic acidosis, aspirin, methanol or ethylene glycol poisoning.
Common Conditions
DKA, kidney failure, severe diarrhea, shock/sepsis, poisoning
Symptoms
Kussmaul respirations, headache, confusion, drowsiness, lethargy, weakness, N/V, abdominal pain, warm flushed skin, hyperK, dysrhythmias, hypotension, coma
Nursing Interventions
Treat the cause (IV insulin + fluids with DKA; dialysis for renal failure; treat diarrhea) Maintain airway; monitor respiratory status. IV fluids + bicarbonate as prescribed. Monitor ABGs, potassium, glucose, ECG, LOC, I&O. Safety precautions; assess for hyperkalemia
ABG:
pH↓ 7.35
CO₂35-45
HCO₃< 22

CO₂: normal, low if compensating

Metabolic Alkalosis

Causes
Acid loss or bicarbonate gain, NG suctioning, vomiting, diuretics (loop + thiazide). hypokalemia, excess antacids or sodium bicarbonate. Excess steroids/Cushing's, hyperaldosteronism, massive blood transfusion.
Common Conditions
prolonged vomiting, NG suctioning, diuretic therapy, antacid overdose, hypokalemia
Symptoms
slow shallow respirations, dizzy, confusion, irritability, numbness & tingling, muscle cramps/twitching, tetany, hyperreflexia, N/V, hypokalemia, hypocalcemia, dysrhythmias, seizures
Nursing Interventions
treat cause (antiemetics for vomiting, review diuretics, Ø antacids) Replace fluid and electrolytes. Monitor ABGs, electrolytes (K, Ca, Cl), ECG, resp. status, I&O, seizure precautions. Encourage potassium rich food.
ABG:
pH> 7.45
CO₂35-45
HCO₃> 26

CO₂: normal, high if compensating
